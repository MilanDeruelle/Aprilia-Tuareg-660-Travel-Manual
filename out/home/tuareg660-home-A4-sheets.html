Daily check

  • Tyres: pressure (cold), cuts, embedded objects, tread.
  • Spokes: tap each with a screwdriver. Even ring = OK; a dull note = loose.
  • Brakes: lever and pedal feel, fluid level, pads, leaks.
  • Oil level (warm, upright) · coolant level (cold).
  • Chain: slack, lube, no tight spots.
  • Throttle snaps back · clutch free play, cable not frayed.
  • Steering free lock to lock, no play, no notches.
  • Side stand springs back; the engine cuts if you select a gear with it down.
  • Fasteners: axles, calipers, racks, sump guard, mirrors.
  • Lights, horn, indicators, brake light; warning LEDs light for 3 s at key-ON.
  • ABS tone wheels clean (mud causes ABS/ATC faults) · fuel, cap locked.

Dust or off-road: check the air filter every few days and the chain daily.

Tyres & wheels

Tubeless puncture plug

  1. Find the leak (soapy water), mark it, pull the object out.
  2. Ream the hole, several strokes.
  3. Load a string plug in the needle (cement if supplied). Push in until ⅓ is left outside, pull the needle straight out.
  4. Trim to about 5 mm, inflate, leak-test.
  5. Ride gently and keep checking the pressure. A plug gets you home: have the tyre repaired properly or replaced.

Front wheel out & in

  1. Lift the front wheel: jack under the sump guard, bike strapped so it can't tip.
  2. Remove both calipers (2 screws each) and hang them up. Don't pull the brake lever while they are off.
  3. Loosen the 2 pinch screws (2), unscrew the axle (1) and pull it out to the left.
  4. Take the wheel out. The spacers (3, 5) come loose: keep each with its side.
  5. Refit: wheel with both spacers, axle from the left. Tighten the axle to 65 Nm first, then the pinch screws to 10 Nm.
  6. Calipers 50 Nm. Pump the lever until firm, spin the wheel.

Rear wheel out & in

  1. Rear stand, or the sump guard on a solid block so the wheel hangs free.
  2. Both sides: loosen the adjuster lock nuts and back the adjusters off.
  3. Remove the axle nut and washer (1).
  4. Support the wheel and pull the axle (10) out to the right; the adjuster blocks (2, 9) come free.
  5. Push the wheel forward, lift the chain off the sprocket and roll the wheel out; the disc slides out of the caliper. Don't press the brake pedal while the wheel is out. Keep the loose spacers (3, 5, 8) with the wheel.
  6. Refit: cush rubbers (6), inner spacer (5), carrier (4) and both outer spacers (3, 8) in place. Wheel in, disc between the caliper pads, chain on.
  7. Axle in from the right with the right adjuster block; then the left block, washer and nut, finger-tight.
  8. Set the chain slack (next chapter), tighten the lock nuts, then the axle nut to 120 Nm.
  9. Pump the brake pedal, spin the wheel, recheck the slack.

Drive chain

Check slack

  1. Engine off, side stand, neutral.
  2. At 320 mm in front of the rear axle, push the bottom run up, then pull it down. Total travel ≈ 48 mm.
  3. Roll the bike and repeat at several points. If the slack varies, links are kinked or seized: replace the chain.

Adjust

  1. Rear stand. Loosen the axle nut, then the lock nuts on both sides.
  2. Turn both adjuster bolts by the same amount. Keep the marks level on both sides and measure that the block-to-swingarm-end distance is the same left and right.
  3. Tighten both lock nuts, then the axle nut to 120 Nm.
  4. Recheck on the side stand. Never set it tighter than spec: the chain tightens as the suspension compresses.

Clean, lube, repair

  • Clean & lube: light water jet, chain cleaner (no petrol or harsh solvents: they kill the O-rings), let it dry, spray the inside of the lower run, let it set. After every wash and after rain.
  • Worn (limit in the Data table), damaged rollers, loose pins, missing O-rings or hooked teeth: replace chain and both sprockets together.
  • Broken chain: fit a 520 master link (a clip link gets you home, a rivet link is permanent) with a chain breaker/riveter. Don't over-press the side plate; the link must move freely.

Brakes

Fluid level & top-up

  • Front: turn the bars until the reservoir is level and read the sight glass. Top up: 2 screws, cover, gasket.
  • Rear: bike upright; screw cap and gasket off; top up with a syringe to between MIN and MAX.
  • With worn pads, top up only to just above MIN, or it will overflow when new pads push the pistons back.
  • DOT 4 absorbs water and strips paint: close it quickly, cover nearby paint. A slow fall is pad wear; a sudden drop is a leak.

Pads

  1. Pull the split pin, slide out the pad pin, take out the pads.
  2. Don't touch the lever or pedal while the pads are out.
  3. Clean the pistons and push them back gently; watch the reservoir level.
  4. New pads, pad pin, new split pin. Pump until firm before riding.
  5. Brake gently for the first ~100 km to bed them in.

Front: always replace the pads in both calipers together.

Bleeding

  1. Pumpthe lever or pedal several times, then hold it
  2. Openthe bleed nipple ¼ turn
  3. Closeit before the lever bottoms
  4. Releasethe lever
  • Bike level, clear hose from the nipple into a bottle. Keep the reservoir above MIN the whole time.
  • Repeat until no bubbles come out. Front: bleed both calipers. Then top up and refit the caps.
  • Still spongy: air is trapped in the ABS unit, which needs the dealer's diagnostic tool.

Lever & pedal

  • Front lever reach: push the lever forward and turn the adjuster; anticlockwise brings it closer.
  • Rear pedal free play: remove the right frame cover, loosen the lock nut, turn the pushrod pin, re-lock.

Oil, coolant, air filter

Oil level

  1. Engine warm from about 15 km of riding (not idling). Switch off and wait a few seconds.
  2. Level ground, bike upright on both wheels, not on the side stand.
  3. Unscrew the dipstick, wipe it, put it back without screwing it in, pull it out and read.
  4. Aim for about MAX. Never below MIN or above MAX. Top up only with the specified oil.

Oil & filter change

  1. Warm engine. Remove the sump guard (2 front screws, then 2 rear while supporting it).
  2. Container of at least 3 L underneath. Drain plug out, then the filler cap; let it drain for several minutes.
  3. Unscrew the old filter. Oil the new filter's seal, pre-fill it about ⅓, screw it in by hand.
  4. Drain plug with a new aluminium washer, 20 Nm.
  5. Fill 2.3 L, run the engine a few minutes, switch off, wait 30 s, top up to MAX.
  6. Check the plug and filter for leaks, refit the sump guard.

Coolant

  • Check cold, bike upright: the level must be between MIN and MAX. Don't ride below MIN.
  • Top up through the expansion tank cap to halfway between MIN and MAX.
  • Never open it hot: the system is pressurised.
  • No coolant to hand: clean water gets you to a workshop. Replace it with the correct mix soon, especially if frost is possible.

Air filter (dust)

Remove the fuel-tank upper cover and the 4 airbox cover screws, lift out the filter. It is paper: tap it out gently from the clean side; never wash or oil it. Replace it if it stays clogged.

Battery, fuses, lights

Battery

  • Remove: key OFF, seats off, cover off. Disconnect − first, then +. Refit + first, then −, with a little grease on the terminals.
  • TEST button on the battery: 5-6 bars ready · 3-4 charge recommended · 1-2 charge needed · 0 faulty.
  • Cold: the lithium battery works from 0 to 40 °C. Below 0 °C, make short repeated start attempts; each one warms it. For long use outside that range fit the lead-acid alternative.

Use a lithium charger, or a plain 12 V charger with no desulphation or pulse mode, never above 15 V. Stop if the battery gets warm.

Jump start: donor car's engine off. + to +, then − to bare metal on the engine or frame. Start, disconnect in reverse order. Never use a booster above 15 V.

Fuses

Key OFF. Check the smallest ratings first and the main fuse last. Replace only with the same rating, and find the cause: a fuse that blows again means a short. Pulling the main fuse resets the clock and trip.

Lights & headlight aim

All lights are LED: no bulbs to carry, a failed module means a new unit.

Aim (after loading luggage): bike 10 m from a wall on level ground, rider seated, low beam on. The cut-off should sit at 0.9 × the headlight's height above the ground: headlight at 90 cm → cut-off at 81 cm.

Low beam: two adjusters behind the fairing (left and right), clockwise lowers. High beam: Phillips screw underneath, clockwise raises.

Suspension & clutch

Adjusting the suspension

Settings and adjuster positions: see Suspension settings in the Data chapter. Turn each adjuster fully clockwise first, then count out. Never force an adjuster past its stop, set both fork legs the same, and add rebound damping when you add preload.

Clutch

Adjust if it slips, drags (the bike creeps or stalls in gear) or bites too late.

  • Fine: the adjuster at the lever; clockwise increases free play.
  • Coarse: the in-line adjuster on the right side; loosen the nut, turn, re-lock.
  • Test: engine running, 1st gear, clutch pulled: no creep, no stall. No slip under acceleration.

After a drop

  • Fuel and oil leaks · levers, pedals and pegs (bend back slowly) · bar alignment.
  • Side-stand switch and spring · radiator and hoses · mirror and handguard bolts.
  • Stand it upright a minute or two before restarting, then check the oil level.
  • Recovery: transport upright, strapped, in 1st gear, mirrors folded. Aprilia advises against towing.

Before the trip

  • Any service due during the trip? Do it before: oil and filter, chain and sprockets, pads, tyres, brake fluid and coolant age.
  • Dry run: remove both wheels and the front pads using only the travel kit. Note every size that didn't fit.
  • Check the drain plug: some 2022-build bikes have the wrong one, which seals only with stacked washers. Correct: OEM 1A012848 with washer 831393 (3P).
  • Set your own immobilizer user code (default 00000, dash menu 6.2) and keep it off the bike. Spare key separate from the bike key.
  • Charge the battery; terminals clean and tight.
  • Fill in My bike: pinch-screw size, drain-plug and bleed-nipple sizes, fuse type.
  • Paint-pen marks across the axle nuts after torquing.
  • Set the suspension for the load, re-aim the headlight.
  • Save the travel PDF and HTML on your phone for offline use. List dealers along the route.

Workshop jobs (not roadside)

  • Spark plugs: fuel tank and complete airbox must come off (WS 37).
  • Valves: bucket tappets with shims 1.75-3.15 mm in 0.025 mm steps (WS 49).
  • Steering head: special tool AP8140190; ring nut 60 Nm, back off, 40 ± 5 Nm; 250 ± 100 g pull at the grip end to turn the bars (WS 148-150).
  • ABS bleeding, fork oil and seals, wheel and steering bearings: dealer.

Washing & storage

  • Engine cold, low-pressure water only. No pressure washer or steam on the seat, electrics, bearings, chain or plastics. Afterwards: test the brakes, lube the chain, levers, pedals, clutch cable and locks (OM 166-170).
  • Parked more than 15 days: pull the main fuse or remove the battery and recharge it monthly (OM 153).

Sources & conflicts

  • Aprilia: owner's manual Ed. 01_10/2021 (2Q000498, OM) and service station manual 2Q000524 (WS).
  • Third-party (3P): rear axle 120 Nm and drain plug 20 Nm from published torque lists (maintenanceschedule.com); tool sizes from Tuareg 660 kits by Eastbound, Obsidian and Tusk; oil filter cross-reference from maintenanceschedule.com; drain-plug issue from AF1 Racing.
  • Oil capacity: spec tables 2.3 L (OM 178, WS 25); WS 40 procedure 2.7 L. 2.3 L matches the Tuareg's shallower sump.
  • Shock settings: WS 158 differs from OM 113 and appears to come from another model; the OM values are used.
  • Pinch clamp side: WS 165 says left fork leg, WS 173 says right; photos and WS 8 show the clamp on the axle-head side (left).
  • Not in your manual edition: rear axle, sprocket, disc and caliper-bracket torques ("prescribed torque" only).

Aprilia may revise specs; your dealer has the latest data. Use at your own risk.
